Project description
The PROSECCO project brings direct advancements to the maturity level of HVDC grids. Doing so, it aids in realizing Europe’s climate goals for 2050. Meshed HVDC grids is a key technology to integrate offshore wind and to upgrade the European power system. As such, the future power system will be a hybrid AC/DC grid where the HVDC grid seamlessly integrates with AC systems. PROSECCO addresses innovation needs in grid protection near HVDC converters and congestion management for hybrid AC/DC grids. More specifically, this project focuses on investigating the following research questions:
Leveraging real-time digital simulators models, how to increase the robustness and confidence of the design of DC protection solutions working toward industrialization, harmonization and on-site demonstration? 
Leveraging real-time digital simulators models, how to develop methodologies to understand and mitigate the impact of faults at the DC and AC side, including study of DC protection strategies and implementations, fault-ride-through strategies, and their co-design for DC and AC system protection.
How to develop novel power flow control approaches to perform security constrained optimal power flow control (SCOPF) in future multiterminal multivendor AC/DC transmission systems to showcase grid reinforcement avoidance through extended CBA analysis?
To address these research questions, PROSECCO intends to overcome key challenges that currently constrain the state of the art. Future meshed HVDC systems will require the design of the first hybrid DC network integrating equipment from multiple vendors. Therefore, the technological solutions must be vendor-agnostic, robust against interactions among diverse devices, and capable of operating in highly dynamic environments that transition between normal operation, fault conditions, and low-voltage ride-through events.
PROSECCO employs Model-Based Systems Engineering (MBSE) to ensure a consistent, vendor-neutral approach from the design stage. More specifically, we focus on exploring novel control techniques that help mitigate faults and, consequently, enhance system reliability. In this project, we leverage robust control, model predictive control, and machine learning methods to design controllers that can adapt to fault conditions and ensure the stability of both individual converters and the overall network. 
Project keywords
meshed hybrid grids, grid protection, congestion management, HVDC. 
Intended deliverables
PROSECCO will mature hybrid AC/DC grids through 4 different demonstrators across three EU member states:
Unique test equipment for DC relays
DC protection relays being installed in actual DC grids
A full-scale DC power flow controller and
Software to evaluate the cost-effectiveness of protection and congestion management solutions.
TU Delft’s role is to lead WP4 and the design of the demonstration of the DC relay and control and protection units in the hardware-in-the-loop real-time RTDS laboratory. Furthermore, the demonstration will be continued in the DC substation.

About the PhD/Postdoc researcher
Victor Daniel Reyes Dreke received his BSc degree in Automation and Control Engineering in 2017 from the Technological University of Havana, Cuba, his MSc degree in System Electronics in 2020 from the Escola Politécnica of University of Sao Paulo, Brazil, and his PhD degree in 2024 from the Electromechanics and Control System Group at the Eindhoven University of Technology, The Netherlands. His main research interests include model-based control of power electronic and energy systems. He is currently a postdoc at Intelligent Electrical Power Grids (IEPG) Group, TU Delft, as part of the PROSECCO project.).
