Project description
The large-scale integration of offshore wind, enabled by voltage source converter (VSC)-based high-voltage direct current (HVDC) technology, is giving rise to hybrid AC–multi-terminal HVDC (MTDC) power systems that combine AC-grid stability with DC-network controllability. The development of hybrid AC-MTDC systems has become a critical area of research due to the increasing integration of renewable energy sources, particularly offshore wind, and the need for flexible, stable, and optimized power delivery. This project develops an optimal power flow (OPF)-integrated coordinated droop control framework that unifies system-level optimization with decentralized converter control while explicitly accounting for wind-generation uncertainty. It addresses:
Research question 1: How to develop an OPF model for a hybrid AC–MTDC system?
Research question 2: How can the local control strategies of VSC converter stations be integrated into the OPF model of hybrid AC–MTDC systems?
Research question 3: How to model the uncertainties arising from offshore wind generation in the control strategy?
Three challenges arise. Formulating an OPF for hybrid AC–MTDC networks requires capturing the nonconvex, tightly coupled AC–DC feasible set and converter limits that standard AC formulations omit. Integrating local droop control must reconcile economic optimality with decentralized autonomy while jointly regulating DC voltage and AC frequency. Finally, offshore wind forecast errors are heteroscedastic and non-stationary, so embedding this uncertainty into a large-scale OPF without sacrificing computational efficiency or real-time feasibility remains difficult.
The work proceeds in three stages. First, an OPF model is developed for hybrid AC–MTDC systems, incorporating VSC/modular multilevel converter (MMC) models and AC/DC network constraints. Second, droop control is embedded through a hierarchical two-stage formulation in which DC-voltage stability precedes generation-cost minimization, with frequency droop loops in the MMCs enabling joint DC-voltage and AC-frequency regulation. Third, wind variability is handled via two complementary extensions: a forecast-integrated anticipatory controller validated by hardware-in-the-loop (HIL)/real-time digital simulator (RTDS) testing, and a chance-constrained stochastic OPF using polynomial chaos expansion (PCE) for closed-form adaptive droop extraction. Validation uses multi-terminal test systems with quantitative benchmarking against baseline forecasts and multiple control modes.
Project keywords

Intended deliverables
An OPF model for hybrid AC–MTDC systems with offshore wind integration. 
A coordinated voltage–frequency droop control strategy embedded in the OPF framework. 
Two uncertainty-aware extensions: a forecast-integrated anticipatory controller and a PCE-based chance-constrained stochastic OPF. 
Real-time HIL/RTDS validation on multi-terminal test systems, benchmarked against a persistence-baseline forecast and multiple control modes. 
A doctoral dissertation and peer-reviewed conference/journal publications.

About the PhD/Postdoc researcher
Hongjin Du is a Ph.D. Researcher in the Intelligent Electrical Power Grids (IEPG) group at TU Delft, where her work centers on the coordinated optimal control of hybrid AC–MTDC systems. Her mission is to develop robust, data-driven strategies that make future power grids more stable and efficient under high-penetration renewable energy. Before her academic career in the Netherlands, she spent four years at CHDER (China Huadian) as a Project Manager and Electrical Engineer, gaining a pragmatic perspective on the real-world constraints of grid operations. She is passionate about the energy transition, grid modeling, and open-source toolchains.
