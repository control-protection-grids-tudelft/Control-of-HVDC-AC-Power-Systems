Project description
The rapid integration of inverter-based resources and converter interface generation (CIG) links into transmission grids is producing converter-dominated systems whose stability behaviour cannot be adequately captured by classical phasor-domain tools, yet full EMT simulation remains computationally costly at network scale. Dynamic phasor (DP) modelling offers a principled middle ground, retaining selected harmonic dynamics through time-varying Fourier coefficients without switching-level resolution. Still, its systematic application to hybrid AC/MTDC systems using a state-space formulation is underdeveloped, and no open-source framework currently unifies DP-based simulation with state-space and harmonic stability assessment.
Research questions:
Can a unified DP-based framework simultaneously support harmonic state-space analysis and impedance-based stability assessment in hybrid AC/DC systems?
 What does the DP transformation uniquely reveal and what does it conceal in the frequency-domain stability assessment of converter-dominated MTDC systems compared to RMS and EMT approaches? 
How do higher-order harmonic interactions and DC-side dynamics affect small-signal stability boundaries in converter-dense MTDC networks, and can participation factor analysis identify the dominant harmonic contributors?
 How can dynamic phasor models of hybrid AC/DC systems be used to systematically identify and compensate dynamics that are invisible to static phasor-based controller design, and what is the resulting improvement in stability margins?
The core difficulty is that the CIG multi-timescale dynamics (arm currents, capacitor balancing, outer control loops) generate harmonic couplings between the AC and DC sides that scalar phasor models miss entirely, hence, deriving algebraically consistent state-space and admittance representations for a full hybrid network without manual per-component derivation requires a systematic symbolic pipeline.
This research adopts dynamic phasor modelling as the core analytical framework, positioning it as the principled middle ground between RMS and full EMT approaches for hybrid AC/DC systems. As known, DPs represent a nearly periodic signal as a sum of time-varying Fourier coefficients rotating at harmonic frequencies , capturing power-electronic dynamics that static phasors discard while avoiding the switching-level resolution that EMT captures. The DQsym formulation transforms these coefficients into slowly varying DQ-frame state variables, enabling a unified state-space representation that simultaneously supports eigenvalue-based small-signal stability analysis and impedance-based harmonic assessment, directly addressing the research gaps. The framework is validated progressively, with EMT simulations in MATLAB/Simulink serving as reference throughout.
Project keywords
Dynamic phasors, Hybrid AC/DC power systems, Harmonic stability analysis, Converter-dominated power systems, Small-signal stability.
Intended deliverables
Full Dynamic Phasor Framework. A comprehensive dynamic phasor (DP)-based modeling framework supporting simulation, control design, and stability analysis for hybrid AC/DC power systems.
Open-source simulation library: A fully validated library implementing the DQsym formulation across a comprehensive component set, with documented mathematical derivations, unit-tested algorithms, and reproducible benchmark results against EMT references across RLC, point-to-point HVDC, and IEEE 9-bus hybrid AC/DC test cases. 
Systematic state-space formulation pipeline: A symbolic-to-numerical workflow for automatically deriving consistent ABCD state-space representations of arbitrary hybrid AC/DC network topologies via Modified Nodal Analysis (MNA), supporting both component-level and system-level linearisation around operating points obtained from power flow solutions. 
Integrated stability assessment procedure: A complete analysis workflow encompassing AC/MTDC optimal power flow for operating point initialization, linearisation and eigenvalue analysis with participation factor decomposition for mode identification; impedance-based harmonic stability assessment, and Bode and Nyquist plots of the feedback transfer function enabling stability assessment of converter-dominated grids.

About the PhD/Postdoc researcher
Saif Alsarayreh received the B.Sc. degree in Electrical Power and Control Engineering from Mutah University, Al-Karak, Jordan, in 2016, and the M.Sc. degree in Electrical Power Systems from the Budapest University of Technology and Economics, Budapest, Hungary, in 2019. He is currently pursuing the Ph.D. degree with the Faculty of Electrical Engineering, Mathematics and Computer Science, Delft University of Technology, Delft, The Netherlands, as part of the CRESYM Harmony project.
Prior to his doctoral studies, he was a Lead HV/LV Power System Engineer with Jaguar Land Rover, where he worked on high- and low-voltage power system design for electric vehicles. His current research focuses on dynamic phasor modeling for stability analysis and control design in converter-dominated power systems, with emphasis on hybrid AC/DC networks and multi-terminal DC (MTDC) systems.
His research interests include mathematical modeling of power systems, stability analysis of modern power systems, and control design.
