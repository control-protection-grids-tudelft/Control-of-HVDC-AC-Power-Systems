Project description
The global transition toward renewable energy and the increasing demand for flexible power transmission have accelerated the deployment of HVDC and hybrid AC/DC systems. Multi-terminal HVDC networks are essential for integrating large-scale renewable energy resources and interconnecting asynchronous grids; however, their operation introduces significant challenges due to complex converter dynamics, evolving system topologies, and the interaction between control and protection systems. Advanced control strategies are required to maintain system stability, coordinate converter operation, and support dynamic grid conditions, while advanced protection schemes are needed to achieve fast, selective, and reliable fault detection and isolation. This research aims to develop advanced control and protection frameworks for future HVDC and hybrid AC/DC systems.
Research question 1: How can advanced DC protection algorithms be developed to achieve fast, selective, and reliable fault detection and isolation in multi-terminal HVDC systems?
Research question 2: How can control and protection coordination strategies enable adaptive performance under changing converter control modes, system topology variations, and dynamic operating conditions?
Research question 3: How can controller hardware-in-the-loop (cHIL) platforms be utilized to validate the performance and resilience of advanced control and protection schemes under realistic HVDC and hybrid AC/DC system scenarios?
The main challenges include the complex interaction between control and protection systems, the fast dynamics of DC faults, and the difficulty of achieving accurate fault detection under varying operating conditions. In addition, the increasing integration of renewable energy sources and power electronic converters introduces significant variability and dynamic behavior in DC systems, requiring advanced control strategies to maintain voltage stability, power flow regulation, converter coordination, and system resilience during normal and disturbed operating conditions. Furthermore, validating the proposed control and protection methods in realistic environments requires advanced simulation and real-time testing platforms.
The project will address these challenges by developing novel control and DC protection algorithms, supported by detailed system modeling, simulation studies, and control hardware-in-the-loop (CHIL) validation. The proposed approaches will focus on improving converter coordination, adaptive system operation, and fast fault detection and isolation. The developed methods will be evaluated under different operating scenarios to assess their effectiveness and enhance the reliability, stability, and performance of future multi-terminal DC power systems.

About the PhD/Postdoc researcher
Rahul received his B.Tech. degree in Electrical Engineering from Veermata Jijabai Technological Institute (VJTI) in Mumbai, India, in 2021. He completed his M.Sc. degree in Electrical Power Engineering at Delft University of Technology in Delft, Netherlands, in 2024. Since August 2024, he has been working as a PhD Researcher at Delft University of Technology in Delft, Netherlands. His research interests include modeling, stability analysis, control, and the application of machine learning in power electronic-dominated power systems.
