Project Description
Power electronic (PE) devices play a crucial role in enabling the integration of renewable energy sources into modern power systems. However, to achieve interoperability of PE devices, barriers are present in technology but also in intellectual property and regulation. Inter-oPEn addresses these challenges through a unique doctoral training program for 10 researchers, combining expertise from both electrical engineering and legal disciplines. To achieve the common goal of the interoperable PE-dominated power system, openness will be a pivotal factor across the different doctoral projects, tackling fundamental aspects of modern PE-based electrical systems such as control, protection, interoperability, governance, and intellectual property challenges. Comprised of 8 academic partners and 13 industrial associated partners, Inter-oPEn offers a broad industry and transmission system operator expertise for doctoral trainings, research, and secondments. Compared with previous EU projects and doctoral training networks focused on interoperable PE-dominated grids, Inter-oPEn introduces two important innovations: first, it explicitly combines technical and legal perspectives; second, it makes openness principles a core foundation of both engineering and legal research, as well as the overall training program.
Objectives: 
Technical and Legal Training for Future PE-Dominated Systems
Industry–Academia Collaboration and Mobility
Innovation-Oriented Research and Practical Engineering Solutions
Scientific and Transferable Skills Development
Project Partners
Description of the research work: 
Tuanku Badzlin Hashfi: studies grid-forming modular multilevel converter-based HVDC systems for future power-electronics-dominated grids. It addresses the issues of internal MMC faults on converter performance and control in HVDC networks. Power Hardware-in-the-Loop experiments are developed and used as a realistic platform to assess the dynamic performance and practical feasibility of the proposed control system.
