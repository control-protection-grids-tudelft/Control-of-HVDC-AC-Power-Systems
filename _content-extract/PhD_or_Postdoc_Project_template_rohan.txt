Project description
The increasing integration of offshore wind power is making modern power systems more converter-dominated, with reduced inertia and weaker grid conditions. MMC-based HVDC networks are essential for connecting offshore wind power to onshore AC grids, but their control becomes challenging during weak-grid operation, short-circuit ratio variations, and fault conditions. This project focuses on developing hybrid control strategies that allow HVDC converters to adapt between grid-following and grid-forming operation to improve system stability and resilience.
• Research question 1: How can hybrid control strategies be developed for MMC-based HVDC converters to ensure stable operation under varying grid conditions?
• Research question 2: How can these strategies improve the capability and resilience of offshore HVDC networks?
The main challenges are the dynamic interactions between converter controls, offshore wind power plants, AC grid strength, and the DC network. These interactions become more critical during weak-grid operation, unbalanced AC faults, DC faults, and rapid changes in operating conditions.
The research addresses these challenges using stability-based switching between grid-following and grid-forming control modes. Polytopic Lyapunov functions are used to define stable operating regions, while real-time simulations and control-hardware-in-the-loop validation are used to assess the practical performance of the proposed control strategies.
Project keywords
HVDC transmission networks, Modular multilevel converters, Hybrid control strategies, Grid-forming control, Grid-following control, Power system stability
Intended deliverables
A developed hybrid control strategy for MMC-based HVDC converters, enabling adaptive operation between grid-following and grid-forming control modes.
• Stability analysis of the proposed control strategy using polytopic Lyapunov functions and stability-based switching criteria.
• Simulation-based validation of the proposed control strategy under weak-grid conditions, short-circuit ratio variations, and AC/DC fault scenarios.
• Real-time simulation and control-hardware-in-the-loop validation to assess the practical feasibility of the proposed approach.

About the PhD/Postdoc researcher
Rohan Kamat Tarcar is a PhD candidate at TU Delft, working on advanced control strategies for offshore HVDC transmission networks. His research focuses on hybrid grid-following and grid-forming control of modular multilevel converter-based HVDC systems, with emphasis on stability, resilience, and real-time validation. He obtained his MSc in Electrical Power Engineering from TU Delft in 2023.
