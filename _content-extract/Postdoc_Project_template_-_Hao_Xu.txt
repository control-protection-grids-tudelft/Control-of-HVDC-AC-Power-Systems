Project description
The rapid growth of renewable generation and power-electronic (PE) converters is reshaping power-system dynamics. Operating at high frequencies with complex switching, PE converters can act as a negative resistance across a wide frequency band and, when interconnected in hybrid AC/DC grids, may drive the system into oscillations, resonances, and instabilities that threaten secure operation. This project develops fast, non-vendor-specific methods to identify such undesired behaviour and to mitigate it, implemented in the open-source Harmony toolbox. It addresses the following research questions:
Research question 1: How can converter-driven instabilities and harmonic resonances in large-scale hybrid AC/DC (multi-terminal, multi-vendor HVDC) power systems be identified accurately and efficiently using impedance-based stability assessment?
Research question 2: Which mitigation measures — for example controller-loop tuning, wideband passivation, or parameter/topology changes — most effectively remove these instabilities while preserving stable, reliable operation, and how can they be automated within the toolbox?
The main challenges are the wide frequency range over which PE converters interact with the grid, which requires a fully dynamic system model; the need to work with black-box, IP-protected vendor converter models; the fragmentation of existing tools across platforms (MATLAB, PSCAD, RSCAD) and the resulting long, multi-tool simulation times; and the lack of unified benchmarks and field-validated datasets for resonance and instability analysis. Achieving accuracy comparable to commercial tools together with dramatically faster execution, while keeping the methods general enough to apply to real TSO networks, is a central difficulty.
To address these challenges, the project builds a mathematical framework for instability and resonance identification and extends it with mitigation and wideband-passivity strategies, implemented in C++ inside the Harmony toolbox for execution in seconds on general-purpose CPUs. Methods are first formulated and tested against the commercial simulator PSCAD (with the Z-tool) and cross-validated with eigenvalue-based analysis in DPsim, then applied to real-life large-scale test cases provided by TenneT. Where useful, machine-learning and adaptive-control elements support parameter estimation and control tuning. Results are benchmarked against defined KPIs and released open-source, together with representative test cases contributed to CRESYM's Collaborative Dynamic Library.
Project keywords
HVDC; power-electronics-based power systems; converter-driven stability; resonance stability; impedance-based stability assessment; harmonic mitigation.
Intended deliverables
A mathematical framework and open-source implementation (C++) for identifying instabilities and resonances in the Harmony toolbox.
Mitigation and wideband-passivity methods integrated into the Harmony toolbox to restore stable operation.
Verification against PSCAD/Z-tool and cross-validation with DPsim on IEEE 9-bus and Nordic benchmark systems.
Application and validation on real-life TenneT test cases, with evaluation of the technical KPIs.
Peer-reviewed journal and conference publications, and public test cases contributed to CRESYM's Collaborative Dynamic Library.

About the PhD/Postdoc researcher
Hao Xu received the B.S. and Ph.D. degrees in electrical engineering from the College of Electrical Engineering, Zhejiang University, Hangzhou, China, in 2019 and 2024, respectively. He is currently a postdoctoral researcher with the Faculty of Electrical Engineering, Mathematics & Computer Science, Delft University of Technology, Delft, Netherlands. His research interests include power system stability analysis and control.
